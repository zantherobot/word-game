# THE QUIET MEGAPHONE
### A Graphic Novel About Getting Heard Without Getting Loud

**Audience:** Middle school (ages 11–14)
**Theme:** Nonviolent Communication (NVC) — Observation, Feeling, Need, Request
**Length:** 16 pages + character sheet + toolbox spread

---

## Characters

- **Maya Rivera** — 7th grader. Curly hair in a high pony. Owns a sketchbook she carries everywhere. Anxious, smart, slow to speak when upset.
- **Jax Kim** — Maya's best friend. Tall, wears a hoodie two sizes too big. Loud laugh, easily embarrassed.
- **Mrs. Oduya** — School counselor. Warm, mid-50s, wears big earrings. Keeps a jar of smooth stones on her desk.
- **Dad (Mr. Rivera)** — Maya's father. Tired eyes, works shifts. Loves Maya fiercely but talks in short sentences.
- **Leo** — Maya's little brother, age 7. Chaotic energy. Always sticky.

## Visual Notes for Illustrator

- **Palette for safe moments:** warm peach + teal, gentle line weight.
- **Palette for conflict / blame:** desaturated blue-gray, jagged panel borders.
- **Motif:** a small megaphone icon shrinks or grows based on whether a character feels heard. Not a plot device — just a feelings barometer in the margin.
- **Thought bubbles:** cloud-shaped, soft white.
- **Speech bubbles:** crisp outline; spikes only when a character is yelling.

## CHAPTER 1 — THE SHOUT

### Page 1 — Cold open

- **Panel 1 (wide):** School hallway at lunch. Kids blur past. Maya stands at her locker, sketchbook clutched to chest. Margin megaphone: medium.
- **Panel 2:** Jax runs up, grinning. "You won't BELIEVE what Trevor said about your comic."
- **Panel 3:** Maya's face falls. Margin megaphone: shrinking.
- **Panel 4 (close up):** Jax, oblivious, still laughing. "He called it 'basically a diary with dragons.'"

### Page 2 — The spark

- **Panel 1:** Maya slams her locker. The sound is a single black word: **CLANG.**
- **Panel 2:** "Why would you TELL me that?!"
- **Panel 3:** Jax, stunned. "I— I thought it was funny—"
- **Panel 4 (wide):** Maya walks away fast. Jax stands alone. Margin megaphone: tiny, upside down.

### Page 3 — Home is not a soft place today

- **Panel 1:** Maya's kitchen. Dad at the stove, back turned. Leo eating cereal at 4pm.
- **Panel 2:** Dad: "Homework?" Maya: "Fine."
- **Panel 3:** Leo, mouth full: "Maya looks maaaad."
- **Panel 4:** Maya stomps to her room. Door slam = jagged panel border.

### Page 4 — Sketchbook spread

- **Full page:** We see Maya's sketchbook open on her bed. She's drawing a warrior with a megaphone for a head. The warrior is screaming but no one around her is listening. In a corner, tiny handwriting: *"How come being loud makes people deaf?"*

## CHAPTER 2 — THE JAR OF STONES

### Page 5 — Called in

- **Panel 1:** Morning. Announcement overhead: "Maya Rivera to Mrs. Oduya's office."
- **Panel 2:** Maya in a hallway chair. Knee bouncing.
- **Panel 3:** Door opens. Mrs. Oduya: "Come on in. Jax is already here."
- **Panel 4:** Jax on the couch, not looking up. Tension: thick line weight.

### Page 6 — The jar

- **Panel 1:** Mrs. Oduya sets a glass jar of smooth stones on the table. "Before we talk, one rule. Only the person holding a stone talks. Everyone else listens."
- **Panel 2:** Jax grabs a stone fast. "She YELLED at me for no reason."
- **Panel 3:** Maya's jaw tight. Hand open toward the jar.
- **Panel 4:** Mrs. Oduya, calm: "Your turn is coming, Maya. I promise."

### Page 7 — The four windows

- **Panel 1:** Mrs. Oduya opens a notebook. Drawn inside: four windows labeled **OBSERVATION — FEELING — NEED — REQUEST.**
- **Panel 2 (inset close-up of notebook):**
    - **Observation:** What actually happened. Like a camera saw it.
    - **Feeling:** What's alive in you.
    - **Need:** What matters to you underneath the feeling.
    - **Request:** A specific, doable thing you'd like.
- **Panel 3:** Jax, confused. "That's it?"
- **Panel 4:** Mrs. Oduya: "That's it. And it's hard."

### Page 8 — The trap word

- **Panel 1:** Mrs. Oduya writes on a whiteboard: **"You always—"** then draws a red X through it.
- **Panel 2:** "Words like *always* and *never* turn an observation into an accusation. The other person stops listening and starts defending."
- **Panel 3:** Maya, quietly: "…oh."
- **Panel 4:** Jax glances at Maya. Margin megaphone on Maya's side: flickering back on.

## CHAPTER 3 — TRYING IT ON

### Page 9 — Practice round

- **Panel 1:** Mrs. Oduya: "Maya, tell Jax what happened. Just the camera version. No 'always,' no 'you're the worst.'"
- **Panel 2:** Maya, careful: "Yesterday at my locker, you told me Trevor called my comic a diary with dragons."
- **Panel 3:** Jax opens his mouth to defend. Mrs. Oduya lifts a finger. "Not yet."
- **Panel 4:** Maya keeps going: "When you said that, I felt embarrassed and kind of small."

### Page 10 — The need underneath

- **Panel 1:** Mrs. Oduya, gentle: "What did you need in that moment, Maya?"
- **Panel 2:** Maya thinks. Thought bubble shows her sketchbook in her hands, like armor.
- **Panel 3:** Maya: "I needed to feel like my art was… safe with you. That you wouldn't pass it around like a joke."
- **Panel 4 (wider):** Jax's face shifts. Not defensive anymore. Just listening.

### Page 11 — The request

- **Panel 1:** Mrs. Oduya: "And a request. Not a demand. Something Jax could actually choose to do."
- **Panel 2:** Maya, almost whisper: "Next time someone says something about my comics, could you check with me before repeating it?"
- **Panel 3:** Jax, holding the stone now: "Yeah. I can do that." Beat. "I'm sorry. I really didn't mean to hurt you."
- **Panel 4:** Maya nods. The jagged edges in the panel borders soften to curves.

### Page 12 — Something shifts

- **Panel 1 (silent):** Mrs. Oduya slides the jar toward the middle of the table.
- **Panel 2 (silent):** Maya and Jax both rest a hand on the jar at the same time.
- **Panel 3:** Margin megaphones for both: medium, upright, open.
- **Panel 4:** Mrs. Oduya: "This won't be the last hard conversation. But now you have the shape of one."

## CHAPTER 4 — THE HARDER ROOM

### Page 13 — Home, later

- **Panel 1:** Maya at the kitchen table. Dad across from her, phone down for once.
- **Panel 2 (thought bubble):** Maya pictures the four windows.
- **Panel 3:** Maya: "Dad. When you ask 'homework?' and walk away before I answer, I feel kind of invisible."
- **Panel 4:** Dad freezes. Not angry — surprised.

### Page 14 — The scary pause

- **Panel 1 (close on Dad's eyes):** A long beat. No words.
- **Panel 2:** Dad: "I didn't know I did that."
- **Panel 3:** Maya: "I think I need, like… two minutes. Where you sit. Even if it's just two."
- **Panel 4:** Dad exhales. "Two minutes. I can do two minutes. Starting now?"

### Page 15 — Two minutes

- **Panel 1 (wide):** They sit. The clock on the wall ticks.
- **Panel 2:** Maya shows Dad her sketchbook. The warrior with the megaphone head.
- **Panel 3:** Dad studies it. "She looks lonely."
- **Panel 4:** Maya: "Yeah. But I'm drawing her a friend next."

## CHAPTER 5 — EPILOGUE

### Page 16 — The quiet megaphone

- **Panel 1:** Later that week. Cafeteria. Jax slides into the seat across from Maya.
- **Panel 2:** Jax: "Observation: you've been drawing for like twenty minutes and haven't eaten your apple."
- **Panel 3:** Maya laughs. "Feeling: judged."
- **Panel 4:** Jax: "Need: a bite of your apple. Request: gimme."
- **Panel 5 (wide final):** Maya rolls her eyes, hands him the apple. Over them, a drawn-in banner of the warrior — this time she's handed the megaphone to a friend. Margin megaphones: full size, both upright.

## TOOLBOX SPREAD (inside back cover)

**THE FOUR WINDOWS — a cheat sheet**

1. **Observation** — *What a camera would see.* No labels, no "always," no "you're the kind of person who…"
2. **Feeling** — *What's alive in you right now.* Sad, scared, tired, hopeful, embarrassed, lonely, relieved. Not "I feel like you're a jerk" — that's a judgment in a trench coat.
3. **Need** — *What matters underneath the feeling.* Safety, respect, rest, honesty, play, belonging, autonomy. Needs aren't demands — everyone has them.
4. **Request** — *A specific, doable thing.* "Could you…" beats "You should…" A request is something the other person is free to say no to. If they can't say no, it's a demand.

**Three reminders from Mrs. Oduya:**
- Anger is a bodyguard for a softer feeling underneath. Ask what it's guarding.
- "You made me feel…" is usually a sign we've skipped a window.
- Listening is half the megaphone.
